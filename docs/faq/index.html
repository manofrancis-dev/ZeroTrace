Scanning basics

What is a URL security scanner?

A URL security scanner checks a web address for evidence of phishing, malware, or other risk. Zero Trace combines reputation data, domain registration information, URL heuristics, and isolated browser behavior into one report. It is a decision aid, not a guarantee.

How does Zero Trace scan a URL?

The backend validates the URL, blocks private and reserved destinations, strips URL credentials, and gathers evidence from VirusTotal and RDAP when configured. An isolated Chromium sandbox records redirects, HTTP status, downloads, and suspicious page signals. The scanner then correlates the findings into a 0–99 evidence-based score. The methodology page explains each stage.

How long does a scan take?

Many scans complete in seconds, but a scan can take longer when VirusTotal performs a fresh analysis, the sandbox is slow, or the backend is waking from a cold start. The progress bar is an estimate, not a measurement. The result is shown only after the backend responds.

Can I scan any website?

You can scan public HTTP and HTTPS URLs. Private, loopback, local, multicast, and reserved destinations are blocked by SSRF protection. Pages that require authentication, block automated browsers, or are only reachable inside a private network may not produce useful evidence. Scans are also rate-limited to protect the service.

Does Zero Trace scan downloaded files?

The sandbox records download events, file names, sizes, and locally computed SHA-256 hashes. Zero Trace does not upload downloaded files to VirusTotal and does not perform a VirusTotal file-hash lookup. A download event is evidence to review, not a malware verdict by itself.

Results and scoring

What do the verdicts mean?

MALICIOUS means a provider or sandbox signal crossed the scanner’s threat threshold. SUSPICIOUS means risk signals were observed but do not prove malware. NO KNOWN THREAT means no finding crossed the configured thresholds in the available evidence; it is not proof that the site is safe.

Is a high safety score a guarantee?

No. The score is an evidence-based summary on a 0–99 scale, not a probability. A high score means fewer threat signals were observed in the available evidence at scan time. It does not guarantee that a site is honest, that it will stay safe, or that it cannot phish you later.

Why is the maximum score 99?

Security evidence is always incomplete. New domains, cloaked pages, and provider gaps mean no scanner can honestly promise 100% safety. Zero Trace intentionally never returns 100 and caps the score when reputation or browser evidence is missing.

What happens when no threat report exists?

The report shows NO_EXISTING_REPORT or a similar coverage state. Zero Trace does not convert missing data into a safe verdict. The score is capped when reputation or browser evidence is unavailable, and the recommendation explains that evidence is limited.

Why do providers show NOT_CONFIGURED or PROVIDER_ERROR?

NOT_CONFIGURED means the provider key or feature is not present on the backend. PROVIDER_ERROR means a provider request failed or was rate-limited. Both are coverage gaps. They are never treated as proof that a URL is safe.

What does confidence mean?

Confidence summarizes how many independent strong threat sources contributed findings. It is High when at least two strong sources did, Moderate when one did, and Limited when none did. It is not a probability and does not replace the provider cards.

Evidence sources

Does Zero Trace use VirusTotal?

Yes, when a VirusTotal API key is configured on the backend. The provider card shows whether VirusTotal was available, whether an existing report was found, and whether a fresh analysis completed. The public API is rate-limited, and submitted or queried URLs may be retained by VirusTotal under its terms.

What does RDAP add?

RDAP provides domain or IP registration context: registration and expiration events, nameservers, and DNSSEC delegation status when published. It helps you judge whether a domain is new or where its DNS is hosted. Registration age is context, not proof of guilt or innocence.

What does the dynamic sandbox do?

An isolated Chromium session opens the URL and records the final URL, redirects, HTTP status, page title, response count, download events, SHA-256 hashes, and suspicious page signals. It observes behavior that a static URL check cannot see. Some sites block automated browsers or serve different content to scanners, so the sandbox is one layer of evidence.

Privacy and safety

Does Zero Trace store submitted URLs?

Zero Trace does not maintain a scan-history database, and scan responses are marked no-store. The URL is still sent to the scanning backend and to third-party services used for the scan. When VirusTotal analysis is enabled, the URL is sent to VirusTotal, whose documentation says submitted or queried items may be added to its dataset. Hosting providers may keep operational logs. Do not scan private or confidential URLs. Read the privacy notes.

Is it safe to scan a suspicious link?

Submitting a URL to Zero Trace does not open it in your browser. The backend and the isolated sandbox fetch it, and the URL may be sent to VirusTotal and RDAP. That is generally safer than opening a suspicious link yourself, but it is not risk-free for confidential URLs. Do not submit private, authenticated, or personal links.

Why can’t I scan private or local URLs?

Zero Trace rejects loopback, private, local, link-local, multicast, and reserved destinations before scanning. This SSRF protection prevents the scanner from being used to probe internal services. Authenticated and intranet pages are out of scope.

Does Zero Trace replace antivirus?

No. Zero Trace is a URL evidence tool, not an antivirus or endpoint protection product. It does not scan your device, monitor downloads continuously, or remove malware. Use it alongside updated software, multi-factor authentication, and a reputable security product.

Using Zero Trace

Is Zero Trace free?

Yes. Zero Trace does not require an account or payment. The scan endpoint is rate-limited to protect the service and third-party provider quotas.

Who built Zero Trace?

Zero Trace is an independent project built and maintained by Mano Francis Dev. It is not affiliated with VirusTotal, Google, or any security vendor.

How can I report a bug or security issue?

For bugs and feature requests, open an issue on the Zero Trace GitHub repository. For a sensitive security report, do not post exploit details publicly; contact the maintainer through the GitHub profile so the issue can be handled privately.
