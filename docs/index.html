What is Zero Trace?

An evidence-first URL security checker

Zero Trace is a free URL scanner for anyone who wants a second opinion before opening a link. It is built for everyday internet users, students, developers, and security researchers who prefer transparent evidence over a vague “safe” or “unsafe” label.

What it does

A live scan, not a static blocklist

When you submit a URL, the backend validates it, checks the destination against SSRF protections, and gathers independent evidence from configured providers. The report shows what was observed, which sources responded, and where coverage is missing.

What it is not

Not a guarantee, and not an antivirus replacement

A scan summarizes available evidence at one point in time. It cannot prove that a website is safe, detect every phishing page, or replace endpoint protection. A high score means fewer threat signals were observed, not that the site is trustworthy forever.

How URL scanning works

Three layers of evidence, one report

Zero Trace does not turn missing provider data into a clean verdict. Each layer answers a different question, and the report keeps those answers visible.

01

Validate and isolate the target

The URL is normalized, limited to HTTP or HTTPS, and checked for private, loopback, link-local, multicast, and reserved destinations. Userinfo is stripped before the URL is sent to providers or the browser sandbox.

02

Collect independent evidence

VirusTotal provides URL reputation and engine detections when configured. RDAP provides registration and nameserver data. An isolated Chromium session records the final URL, redirects, HTTP status, downloads, and suspicious page signals.

03

Correlate, cap, and explain

Findings and provider states are combined into a 0–99 evidence score. Missing reports and provider errors reduce coverage instead of counting as safe. The verdict and recommendation explain what the evidence supports.

Website safety basics

How to check whether a website is safe

A scanner is one part of the decision. These five checks help you judge a link before you enter a password, payment detail, or personal information.

01

Read the domain from right to left

The registrable domain is the part just before the first single slash. In paypal.com.account-check.example, the actual domain is example, not PayPal. Subdomains and extra words can be chosen by anyone.

02

Look for lookalike characters and punycode

Numbers, swapped letters, hyphenated brand names, and internationalized domains can imitate a trusted site. A hostname containing xn-- uses punycode and deserves extra scrutiny.

03

Treat urgency and credential requests as red flags

Unexpected password resets, failed payments, delivery fees, and account-closure warnings are common phishing themes. Do not enter credentials on a page you reached from an unsolicited message.

04

Check evidence, not just the padlock

HTTPS encrypts the connection; it does not prove who owns the site. Use reputation, registration data, redirect behavior, and download analysis together. A new domain with a valid certificate can still be a phishing page.

05

Scan before you click, and act quickly if you already did

Run the link through Zero Trace before opening it. If you already clicked, do not enter data, close the page, and check the device. If credentials were entered, change them and contact the service directly.

URL security analysis

What the scanner examines

Each source contributes different evidence. The report keeps provider status visible so you can see what was actually checked.

Reputation

VirusTotal URL reports

Existing URL reports, engine detections, and — when enabled — a bounded fresh analysis. A missing report is shown as missing, never as clean.

Registration

RDAP domain data

Registration and expiration events, nameservers, and DNSSEC delegation status for the domain or IP address behind the URL.

Behavior

Isolated browser analysis

A sandboxed Chromium session records the final URL, redirect count, HTTP status, page title, response count, downloads, and suspicious page signals.

Structure

URL and phishing heuristics

HTTP-only links, credential-style @ syntax, unusual length, punycode, account-lure terms, redirect parameters, IP hosts, executable paths, and lookalike brand tokens.

Correlation

Evidence-based scoring

Findings are combined into a 0–99 score with coverage caps. When reputation or browser evidence is unavailable, the score cannot present sparse data as near-certain safety.

Guardrails

Security boundaries

Private, loopback, link-local, multicast, and reserved destinations are rejected. URL credentials are stripped before external requests. Scan responses are marked no-store and requests are rate-limited.

Reading the report

Understanding scan results

The verdict summarizes the evidence. The provider cards tell you how much evidence was actually available. Read both.

Zero Trace verdicts and provider states
Result What it means What to do
MALICIOUS A provider detection or sandbox signal crossed the scanner's threat threshold. This is decisive observed evidence, not a probability. Do not open the link or download files from it. Treat it as unsafe unless independently verified in a controlled environment.
SUSPICIOUS Risk signals were observed, but they do not prove malware. Examples include suspicious URL structure or a small number of suspicious engine results. Avoid entering credentials or payment details. Verify the destination through an official app or a known address.
NO KNOWN THREAT No finding crossed the configured thresholds in the evidence available at scan time. This is not proof that the site is safe. Continue with normal caution. Check the domain and context before sharing sensitive information.
NO_EXISTING_REPORT VirusTotal had no usable report for this URL. Missing data is a coverage gap, not a clean result. Treat the score as less certain. Rely on the other evidence and your own checks.
NOT_CONFIGURED / PROVIDER_ERROR A provider key is absent, a provider failed, or a request was rate-limited. The report says so instead of guessing. Interpret the result with lower confidence. Retry later if the scan is important.
Score 0–99 An evidence summary, not the probability that a URL is safe. A score of 100 is intentionally never returned as a guarantee. Use the score with the verdict, findings, confidence, and provider coverage — not on its own.
Why the score stops at 99. Security evidence is always incomplete. New domains, cloaked pages, and provider gaps mean no scanner can honestly promise 100% safety. Zero Trace caps the score when reputation or browser evidence is missing so that sparse coverage cannot look like certainty.

Before you click

Why you should check suspicious links

Phishing and malware campaigns rely on a moment of trust. A link can arrive through email, SMS, a messaging app, a social post, a search ad, or a QR code.

HTTPS is not a trust signal

Any site can obtain a certificate. Encryption protects the connection to the page; it says nothing about who operates the page or what it will do with your credentials. Phishing pages routinely use HTTPS and can copy a login screen closely.

Context matters as much as the domain

An unexpected “payment failed” message, a delivery fee for a parcel you did not send, or a login alert you did not trigger is a reason to pause. Open the official app or type the known address instead of using the link.

A scan adds evidence before you authenticate

Reputation, registration age, redirects, and browser behavior can reveal risk that is invisible in the address bar. The report also shows when evidence is missing, so you can decide how much confidence to place in it.

Some risks appear only after the click

Redirect chains, fake download pages, and drive-by behavior are easier to observe in an isolated browser than in a static URL. That is why Zero Trace includes a sandboxed browser layer instead of relying on a blocklist alone.

Honest limits

What a scan cannot tell you

Zero Trace is designed to show its limits, not hide them. Keep these constraints in mind when you read any result.

  • No scanner is perfect. A new, cloaked, or targeted page may have no report and no obvious signal at scan time.
  • Only public HTTP/HTTPS URLs are scanned. Private, loopback, local, multicast, and reserved destinations are blocked. Authenticated or intranet pages are out of scope.
  • Sites can behave differently for scanners. Some block automated browsers, show different content, or redirect based on location, device, or reputation.
  • Provider coverage can change. VirusTotal and RDAP may be unavailable, rate-limited, or missing a report. Those states reduce confidence.
  • No report is not proof of safety. Missing evidence is treated as missing, never as a clean verdict.
  • The score is not a probability. It summarizes observed evidence on a 0–99 scale. 100 is intentionally never returned.
  • Do not scan confidential URLs. When VirusTotal analysis is enabled, the URL is sent to VirusTotal, whose documentation says submitted or queried items may be added to its dataset.

Common questions

Zero Trace FAQ

Short answers to the questions people ask before running a URL security scan.

What is a URL security scanner?

A URL security scanner checks a web address for evidence of phishing, malware, or other risk. Zero Trace combines reputation data, domain registration information, URL heuristics, and isolated browser behavior into one report. It is a decision aid, not a guarantee.

How can I check if a website is safe?

Read the domain carefully, look for lookalike characters, treat unexpected credential requests as suspicious, and check independent evidence instead of relying on the padlock. You can paste a public URL into the scanner for a live report. Also consider the context: an unexpected message is a risk signal even if the domain looks familiar.

Can Zero Trace detect phishing websites?

It can surface phishing-related evidence: credential-lure terms, lookalike brand tokens, punycode, redirects, suspicious browser behavior, and VirusTotal detections. It cannot confirm every phishing page, especially a new or cloaked site with no report. A “no known threat” result is not a clean bill of health.

Does Zero Trace use VirusTotal?

Yes, when a VirusTotal API key is configured on the backend. The provider card shows whether VirusTotal was available, whether an existing report was found, and whether a fresh analysis completed. The public VirusTotal API is rate-limited, and submitted or queried URLs may be retained by VirusTotal under its terms.

What happens when no threat report exists?

The report shows NO_EXISTING_REPORT or a similar coverage state. Zero Trace does not convert that into a safe verdict. The score is capped when reputation or browser evidence is unavailable, and the recommendation tells you that evidence is limited.

Is a high safety score a guarantee?

No. The score is an evidence-based summary on a 0–99 scale, not a probability. A high score means fewer threat signals were observed in the available evidence at scan time. It does not guarantee that a site is honest, that it will stay safe, or that it cannot phishing you later.

Does Zero Trace store submitted URLs?

Zero Trace does not maintain a scan-history database, and scan responses are marked no-store. The URL is still sent to the scanning backend and to third-party services used for the scan. When VirusTotal analysis is enabled, the URL is sent to VirusTotal, whose documentation says submitted or queried items may be added to its dataset. Hosting providers may keep operational logs. Do not scan private or confidential URLs.

Can I scan any website?

You can scan public HTTP and HTTPS URLs. Private, loopback, local, multicast, and reserved destinations are blocked by SSRF protection. Pages that require authentication, block automated browsers, or are only reachable inside a private network may not produce useful evidence. Scans are also rate-limited to protect the service.
